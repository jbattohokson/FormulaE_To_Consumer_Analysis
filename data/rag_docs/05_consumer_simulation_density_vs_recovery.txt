Consumer EV Simulation — Density vs Recovery Strategy
Source: FE2C Technical Report — Sections 6.1, 6.2, 6.3

THE CONSUMER EV SIMULATION PHASE

The simulation phase applies Formula E battery management findings to real-world consumer
EV challenges, using California as the primary market context due to its EV adoption leadership,
diverse driving environments, and well-documented range anxiety patterns.

THE CORE SCENARIO (Section 6.1)

If Formula E-derived regen efficiency data is mapped to California traffic patterns, which battery
management strategy produces the greatest consumer range improvement:

- Increasing battery density (larger pack), or
- Increasing recovery efficiency (faster and more effective regenerative braking)?

CALIFORNIA'S TWO CHALLENGE ENVIRONMENTS

Inland Empire / Central Valley:
High ambient temperatures stress thermal management. Stop-and-go traffic on I-10 and SR-99
creates frequent regen opportunities. Range anxiety is acute due to sparse charging
infrastructure relative to distances traveled. This environment favors Battery Density strategy.

Bay Area / LA Metro:
High traffic density on I-405, I-80, and Highway 101 produces dense regen cycles. Shorter
average trip distances reduce range anxiety but increase charging frequency demands.
This environment favors Recovery Efficiency strategy.

The contrast between these two environments is why the FE2C ZIP code selection includes both:
the inland corridor provides the thermal stress test; the Bay Area/coastal corridor provides the
regen-dense urban use case where Formula E learning is most directly applicable.

REGEN PROXY MODELING (Section 6.2)

Because Formula E does not publish raw regen telemetry, a Regen Opportunity Index is
constructed from available data:

- Deceleration zone frequency per lap (from sector time analysis)
- Lap time variance as a proxy for energy recovery variability across racing conditions
- Generation-adjusted regen capacity based on published Gen 3 front axle regeneration
  specifications (250 kW front, supplementing rear powertrain)

The Regen Opportunity Index formula used in this project:
  regen_opportunity_index = (1 / lap_time_variance) * log1p(elevation_delta_m) / battery_kwh

This normalizes by pack size so Gen 2 (52 kWh) and Gen 3 (38.5 kWh) teams are compared
on equivalent terms — a higher index means more regen opportunity per unit of stored energy.

DENSITY VS. RECOVERY BUSINESS OUTPUT (Section 6.3)

Battery Density Strategy:
Larger pack, higher upfront cost, heavier vehicle, longer range buffer.
Favored in low-infrastructure markets (Central Valley long-haul).
Lucid Air Grand Touring (516 mi EPA, 118 kWh) as the primary benchmark.

Recovery Efficiency Strategy:
Optimized regen, lighter pack, lower cost, shorter range but more dynamic charging.
Favored in dense urban stop-and-go (LA Metro, Bay Area).
Aligns with Formula E Gen 3 design philosophy (600 kW combined regen capability).

Hybrid Recommendation:
ZIP code-level California adoption data used to weight the recommendation:
- Dense urban ZIPs (median EVs per ZIP >= 500): favor Recovery Efficiency
- Rural and exurban ZIPs: favor Battery Density

This threshold-based recommendation is the primary business deliverable of the FE2C Week 3
simulation — it translates racing engineering data into a market strategy insight.

LUCID CONSUMER BENCHMARK CROSSWALK

The FE2C regen transfer simulation estimates how the focus team's (Mahindra's) Gen 3 regen
advantage translates to real-world range on Lucid consumer vehicles:

Transfer factor = (focus_regen_index - field_regen_index) / field_regen_index
(can be negative; the CI resamples both groups. Race stints are simulated, so this
demonstrates the method, not a real advantage.)
Regen weight = 15% (conservative midpoint of real-world regen contribution to range)
Simulated range = EPA_baseline * (1 + transfer_factor * regen_weight)

Applied to each Lucid model:
- Air Sapphire (427 mi EPA): performance flagship
- Air Grand Touring (516 mi EPA): primary density benchmark
- Gravity SUV (450 mi EPA): demonstrates architecture scaling to SUV platform
